EHPAD billing compliance: challenges, obligations and optimization levers for directors

EHPAD billing compliance is today one of the most sensitive issues for directors of residential care facilities for dependent elderly people (EHPAD — Établissement d'Hébergement pour Personnes Âgées Dépendantes). Between CPAM (Caisse Primaire d'Assurance Maladie — Primary Health Insurance Fund) requirements, the pricing rules of the Departmental Council, FIDES (Facturation Individuelle Des Établissements de Santé — Individual Billing for Healthcare Facilities) reporting and the multiplicity of funding bodies, the slightest billing discrepancy can generate rejections, overpayments or penalties. Yet many facilities continue to manage this process manually, risking fragile compliance that is costly in terms of human time.

Understanding the regulatory framework of EHPAD billing

EHPAD billing is based on a three-part structure: the care package covered by Health Insurance, the dependency package co-financed by the Departmental Council through the APA (Allocation Personnalisée d'Autonomie — Personalized Autonomy Allowance), and accommodation costs borne by the resident or social assistance. Each of these flows follows its own rules, with distinct stakeholders, deadlines and transmission formats. Mastering these three components is the foundation of any compliance approach.

Regulations evolve regularly. The EHPAD funding reform, undertaken as part of the Social Security Financing Act (loi de financement de la Sécurité sociale), is progressively strengthening the requirements for traceability and justification of billed procedures. The rollout of FIDES in EHPADs, still being extended, requires dematerialized and individualized transmission of billing data to Health Insurance, which demands increased rigor in maintaining resident records and coding procedures.

The main risks of non-compliance and their consequences

Billing errors in EHPADs are rarely intentional, but they can have serious consequences. Health Insurance rejections, often linked to anomalies in social security numbers, inconsistencies between the declared GIR (Groupe Iso-Ressources — Iso-Resource Group, the French dependency classification) and the level of care billed, or exceeded package limits, generate cash flow delays that can amount to tens of thousands of euros. Overpayments, meanwhile, can lead to reimbursements imposed by the CPAM, sometimes with penalties.

Beyond rejections, non-compliance exposes the facility to in-depth audits by ARS (Agences Régionales de Santé — Regional Health Agencies) or Health Insurance services. These audits mobilize significant internal resources and can result in remediation plans. In a context where EHPAD margins are under pressure, non-compliant billing is not merely a regulatory risk: it is a direct economic risk.

The most common errors observed in the field

  • Incorrect allocation of procedures between the care package and external private billing
  • Failure to update the GIR when revising the PATHOS/AGGIR assessment
  • Discrepancy between the resident's actual admission date and the billing start date
  • Coding errors for medications under the global allocation
  • Failure to update AMC (Assurance Maladie Complémentaire — Supplementary Health Insurance) rights when the resident changes mutual fund

Structuring an effective internal control process

The first step for a director seeking to secure EHPAD billing compliance is to precisely map their billing flows. Who enters what? In which HIS (Hospital Information System) or clinical software? How often is data verified before transmission? This mapping often reveals gray areas or instances of double entry that are potential sources of error.

A monthly dashboard integrating the rejection rate by type of service, the average processing time for rejections and the volume of detected overpayments enables proactive compliance management. This oversight must involve both the administrative management and the care teams, since many billing errors originate from incomplete or late clinical information: an unreported discharge, a change of attending physician, an untransmitted prescription.

The key role of team training

Staff responsible for billing must be regularly trained on regulatory changes. The procedure nomenclature, package thresholds, accumulation rules: all of these change every year in Health Insurance circulars. Establishing a formalized regulatory watch shared with teams is a best practice that significantly reduces coding errors.

Automation in the service of compliance: what AI agents make possible

Faced with the complexity and volume of verification tasks, an increasing number of facilities are exploring the contribution of artificial intelligence. AI agents capable of operating software interfaces — what is known as browser-use technology — open concrete possibilities for EHPAD billing compliance, without requiring any specific IT development or software migration.

In practice, an AI agent can, every night, open the facility's HIS, cross-reference the list of present residents with the transmitted billing data, identify discrepancies (discharged resident not deregistered, GIR not updated for more than 6 months, expired AMC rights), then generate an anomaly report directly usable by the billing department the following morning. The same agent can connect to the Viamedis or Almerys portals to verify a resident's supplementary health insurance rights in real time, and automatically report the coverage rate in the billing software — without any administrative staff member needing to intervene manually. This type of automation, deployable within 48 hours on existing tools, transforms compliance monitoring from a burdensome task into a smooth and systematic process.

The goal is not to replace teams, but to spare them repetitive, low-value verification tasks so they can focus on handling genuine anomalies and managing relationships with funding bodies. In facilities where this type of solution has been deployed, the rejection rate has decreased significantly, freeing up valuable administrative time for higher-priority work.
